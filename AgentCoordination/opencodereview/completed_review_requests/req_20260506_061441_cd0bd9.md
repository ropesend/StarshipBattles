## Instructions

PROJ-368 is the largest production refactor in this sequence — `OrderProcessor` collapsed from 910 to 168 LOC by extracting 9 handlers behind an `IOrderHandler` protocol. Focus areas in priority order:

1. **Phase 4 atomicity correctness.** The deletion phase removed ~700 LOC of legacy private methods atomically while switching dispatch to handlers. Walk through `order_processor.py` (now 168 LOC) and confirm:
   - Every public method that previously dispatched to a private `_process_*` / `_execute_*` helper now delegates to the registry.
   - No private helpers were left behind. The AST guard `test_order_processor_no_legacy_helpers.py` asserts this; verify the test is sound (it doesn't have an overly-broad allowlist that would let a legacy helper sneak through).
   - The registry-completeness test `test_handler_registry_completeness.py` asserts every `OrderType` has a handler. Verify the test enumerates `OrderType` correctly and the assertion can't be bypassed by a stub handler.

2. **Handler semantic equivalence.** For each extracted handler, was the new implementation semantically identical to the deleted private method? Specifically:
   - **JoinFleetHandler** (`join_fleet.py`): the 3-phase pipeline (Phase A collect, Phase B mutual-pair canonicalize, Phase C aliveness re-validate) for BUG-122. Walk through the agent's port and confirm it matches the deleted `_process_join_fleet_orders` logic.
   - **ColonizeHandler** (`colonize.py`): port of `process_colonize` + `_deploy_drop_pod`. The agent recorded a quirk (`fleet_consumed=True` mirrors legacy `result.colonized`). Verify this preserves the original public-facing return shape.
   - **SelfDestructHandler** (`self_destruct.py`): lifted from `superweapon_order_processor.py:664-740`. Confirm it behaves identically and that nothing in `superweapon_order_processor.py` (now 708 LOC) still references the lifted code.
   - **TransferHandler** (`transfer.py`): 7 explicit `_dispatch_*` branches (3 load × cargo type, 3 unload × cargo type, fleet↔fleet). For each branch, verify the dispatch decision matches the deleted `process_transfer` switch ladder.
   - **SuperweaponHandlerAdapter** × 5: thin adapters around `superweapon_order_processor.execute_superweapon`. Verify the adapter pattern is consistent across all 5 (no asymmetric handling).

3. **Test migration soundness.** The agent reported migrating ~30 pre-existing characterization tests that reached into now-deleted private methods. Pick 3-5 of those migrations and verify:

4. **Public surface preservation.** OrderProcessor is called from `turn_engine.py`, `strategy_session_facade.py`, and `superweapon_order_processor.py`. Verify:

6. **Anything else.** Look for: layering violations (handler reaching into UI?), broad `except Exception` without rationale comment, missing return-type annotations, files approaching 500 LOC.
